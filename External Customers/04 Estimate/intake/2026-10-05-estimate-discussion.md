## Current assessment-to-estimate work

Gavin said he personally performs all onsite assessments.

He uses the property, photos and videos, and his experience to estimate task time mentally by area and service, recording the time estimates in notes. He then sums those task times for the work being priced.

This describes the present judgment-based practice. It does not establish a measured production-time standard or a completed assessor scoring system.

## Keep three parts of the estimate separate

### 1. Time and price baseline

The calculation Gavin described is:

**Baseline per-visit price = summed estimated hours per visit × quoting rate**

**Baseline monthly amount = baseline per-visit price × agreed visit count ÷ 12**

The monthly calculation describes the typical 12-month arrangement discussed previously. No corresponding formula for a nonannual arrangement was established.

A hypothetical example discussed was:

- Mowing: **75 minutes**
- Trimming: **30 minutes**
- Blowing: **15 minutes**
- Total: **120 minutes, or 2 hours**
- At an illustrative **$50 per hour**, the baseline is **$100 per visit**

The $50 rate and example are illustrative, not a live rate or a new customer quote.

### Reported visit counts

Gavin's latest reported counts in the discussion were:

- Monthly service: **9 visits**
- Biweekly service: **17 visits**
- Weekly service: **37 visits**

These are the counts he reported, not counts inferred from the calendar. The agreed visit count for the particular arrangement remains relevant to its calculation. These notes do not rewrite historical agreements or authorize changing an existing customer's visit count.

### 2. Discretionary monetary adjustment

After the baseline calculation, Gavin may adjust the monthly price using factors such as:

- The customer interaction
- Route density
- Whether the property is in a desired area
- Turf characteristics
- His general judgment about the opportunity

This is reported discretion, not a fixed adjustment schedule or an approved scoring rule. A monetary adjustment should not be confused with changing the estimated task-time baseline.

### 3. Rounding

Gavin said he generally rounds monthly amounts **up**:

- Maintenance: to the next **$10** increment
- Fertilization and pre-emergent treatments: to the next **$5** increment

Rounding is a separate step from time estimation and discretionary monetary adjustment. This records his described practice; no pricing automation or exact rounding implementation was approved.

## Proposed future assessor ratings

Gavin discussed a possible **1–10 rating** of assessor criteria to support more consistent adjustments.

This remains a proposal. No rubric, criteria list, weights, score-to-price conversion, or automatic adjustment was established. It should not be treated as a completed tool or an instruction to build one.
