## Direction stated by Gavin

### Lead and qualified lead

- A **lead** is anyone who can be contacted.
- A **qualified lead** is contactable and interested.
- Further fit criteria need to be discussed and standardized. Location is one such criterion.
- The evidence that establishes interest, and the additional criteria for deciding which interested leads to pursue, remain open.

Contactability describes the lead definition. It does not establish permission to contact someone or remove applicable contact restrictions.

### Geographic direction

- Over roughly the next three years, Gavin would like **at least 90% of revenue to come from Comanche Trace**.
- This is an aspiration, not a measured baseline, an adopted performance requirement, or a fixed implementation deadline.
- Gavin described Comanche Trace as a developing community with room to build future customer clusters.
- The desired service pattern is a compact cluster where an employee can park once and work through a service day.
- Driving efficiency matters. Location should help build this pattern rather than merely place more customers somewhere within the same community.
- High-value customers outside Comanche Trace remain worth considering. The geographic preference is not a hard exclusion rule.

### A tradeoff tested in the discussion

Gavin answered yes to prioritizing an isolated Comanche Trace prospect over an equally profitable prospect next to an existing customer elsewhere.

This records the preference in that specific comparison. It does not establish how much profitability, travel time, capacity, or other fit to sacrifice in different cases.

## Provisional ideas discussed

These are discussion proposals, not approved rules or final rankings.

### Three possible location tiers

1. **Comanche Trace, adjoining a compatible existing cluster.**
2. **Other Comanche Trace locations that could seed a new cluster.**
3. **Outside Comanche Trace, considered selectively for profitability and route fit.**

The tier structure was acknowledged in conversation. Definitions, exceptions, weights, and thresholds are still unresolved. It should not be treated as an automatic qualification or rejection system.

### What makes a cluster workable?

The discussion considered more than vehicle driving:

- Walking and moving equipment between properties
- Loading, unloading, setup, and other handling
- Travel to and from the shop
- Compatible service days, frequencies, and customer scheduling constraints

A nearby property may still be difficult to serve in the same working block. How to compare these factors has not been decided.

## Questions still to work through

The questions below are prompts for further discussion, not requirements or commitments. Keep only criteria that change a real pursuit, scheduling, or service decision.

### 1. Service fit

- Which services are wanted, and which can the business reliably deliver?
- How should recurring maintenance compare with one-time work?
- Are there requests that should be declined or referred regardless of location?
- What must be known at first contact, and what can wait until assessment?

### 2. Economics and exceptions

- What does “equally profitable” mean for comparisons: contribution per visit, per labor hour, over a season, or another measure?
- Which travel, setup, handling, and service costs should be included?
- What makes an outside-Comanche-Trace opportunity valuable enough to pursue?
- How should a promising future cluster be weighed against today's route efficiency?
- Are any minimum economic thresholds useful? None have been set.

### 3. Schedule and capacity

- Can the requested frequency and timing fit an existing service day?
- How much schedule flexibility is needed to preserve a compact cluster?
- What happens when a good-fit lead wants service before capacity is available?
- Which information is needed to distinguish a current opportunity from one to revisit later?

### 4. Property and execution fit

- Do access, terrain, parking, equipment movement, or service scope materially change the work?
- Can the property be served safely with the available equipment and skills?
- What makes two nearby properties operationally compatible?
- Which details need a visit rather than an initial conversation?

### 5. Interest and readiness

- What evidence is enough to call someone interested?
- Are they exploring options, asking for an assessment, or ready to buy?
- Is the contact able to authorize the requested work, or is another decision-maker involved?
- When should a lead move to the next lifecycle stage, and what is that stage?
- How should qualification differ from priority? An interested lead may still be a low-priority opportunity.

### 6. Expectations and working relationship

- Are the requested service standard, scope, communication, and schedule expectations compatible?
- What needs to be explained early to avoid an unsuitable estimate or an avoidable mismatch?
- Which concerns should prompt clarification rather than immediate rejection?

### 7. Minimal information and learning

- What is the smallest useful set of facts needed to choose the next step?
- Where should those facts live, who acts next, and how do we avoid collecting the same information twice?
- Which evidence would show whether the location strategy improves revenue mix and service efficiency?
- How should the revenue-share measure be defined, including its time period?
- What baseline exists today? None was supplied in this discussion.

No scoring system, mandatory form, numeric cutoff, response-time target, pilot, or automation has been approved.
